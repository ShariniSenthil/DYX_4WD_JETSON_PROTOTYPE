### How "stationary" was decided — and why it is not circular

Windows come from **actuator output and gyro only**: `actuator_motors.control[0..1]`
below 0.01 (only two channels are driven; `control[2..5]` are identically zero)
and `vehicle_angular_velocity.xyz[2]` below 0.02 rad/s. **Neither GNSS nor the
EKF is consulted**, so measuring their scatter inside those windows does not
assume its own conclusion. This satisfies CLAUDE.md's rule *"never judge
stopped-ness from the EKF velocity"* structurally rather than by discipline,
and it also avoids the second documented trap — position-derived speed cannot
tell parked from pivoting-in-place, but a gyro gate can.

Two further filters, both of which turned out to matter:

- **head trim (`--settle`, default 2.0 s)** — the estimator rings after a pivot;
- **linear-drift rejection (`--max-drift`, default 20 mm)** — a monotonic ramp
  inside a nominally stationary window is leftover motion, not receiver noise.
  One window (`log_87`) held a **59 mm east ramp** with the drivetrain already
  neutral. Leaving it in inflated the pooled horizontal sigma from 2.6 mm to
  **6.2 mm**, i.e. it would have more than doubled the headline number.

`log_77` contributes nothing: its longest motor-neutral stretch is 3.13 s, below
the 2.0 s trim + 2.0 s minimum. The in-mission marking hold is only
`marking_hold_sec: 3.0`, so most mission stops are marginal for this method —
the bulk of the data comes from longer pre/post-mission parked periods.

## 2. Results — the seven quantities Task 2.1 asks for

Residual about each window's own mean (so the earth model and any absolute
offset cancel), pooled across all windows. For velocity the true value is zero
while parked, so the residual **is** the error.

| quantity | measured sigma | p95 \|·\| | max \|·\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| raw GNSS horizontal (2D) | **2.6 mm** | 4.4 mm | 7.5 mm |
| — north / east | 1.7 / 1.9 mm | | |
| raw GNSS vertical (MSL and ellipsoid identical) | **4.8 mm** | 9.3 mm | 16.6 mm |
| raw GNSS velocity N / E / D | **7.9 / 8.3 / 10.6 mm/s** | 15.9 / 16.0 / 20.4 mm/s | 32.6 / 42.7 / 48.5 mm/s |
| raw GNSS yaw (dual-antenna) | **0.128°** | 0.26° | 0.43° |
| EKF local position x / y / z | **2.1 / 3.1 / 5.7 mm** | 4.3 / 5.8 / 11.1 mm | 18.2 mm |
| EKF local velocity x / y / z | **4.3 / 4.7 / 4.7 mm/s** | 8.4 / 9.3 / 10.3 mm/s | 15.1 / 23.5 / 15.5 mm/s |
| EKF yaw | **0.0095°** | 0.02° | 0.03° |

Robustness: raw GNSS is essentially **insensitive to the settle trim**
(2D sigma 2.8 / 2.6 / 2.7 mm at settle = 1 / 2 / 3 s) — the receiver has no
post-stop transient. The **EKF is not**: local x/y go 4.5/8.0 → 2.1/3.1 →
2.1/2.8 mm across the same sweep. That is the settling transient of §5, seen
through a second, independent statistic.

### Bag-side cross-check (13 bundles, what the controller consumes)

Gated on `/rpp/command_speed_mps == 0` (command-side, also non-circular), decoded
with the verified reader in `scripts/analyze_mission.py`:

| quantity | bag (13 runs) | ulog (8 runs) | agrees? |
|---|---|---|---|
| EKF position, 2D RMS | 4.0 mm | ~3.8 mm | yes |
| EKF velocity, 2D RMS | 6.3 mm/s | 6.4 mm/s | yes |
| raw GNSS horizontal, 2D RMS | 5.4 mm | 2.6 mm | see below |
| raw GNSS altitude sigma | 6.2 mm | 4.8 mm | yes |

The horizontal gap is **quantization, not disagreement**. `GPSRAW` carries
lat/lon as `int32 × 1e-7 deg` = an 11.06 mm quantum, whose own sigma is
11.06/√12 = **3.19 mm**; 2.6 mm ⊕ 3.19 mm = 4.1 mm, against 5.4 mm measured
across a partly different run set. The ulog's `vehicle_gps_position.latitude_deg`
is a `double` and carries no quantum — **prefer the ulog for any GNSS position
noise figure.**

### ⚠ Correction to a number in CLAUDE.md

CLAUDE.md records a **"stationary noise floor of median 0.0233 / p99 0.0535 m/s"**
for the EKF velocity. Measured here with an actuator+gyro gate and a settle trim,
the parked floor is **6.3 mm/s 2D RMS (bags) / 6.4 mm/s (ulog)** — about 3.7×
smaller.

These are not in conflict; they measure different things. The 23 mm/s figure was
gated by position-derived speed, which that same document later established
**cannot separate parked from pivoting** and which necessarily includes the
post-pivot ringing. So 23 mm/s is the **ringing**, and ~6 mm/s is the **floor**.
The 2026-09-01 decision to raise the pivot-settle gate 0.030 → 0.060 m/s was
about the ringing and **remains correct** — nothing here argues for reverting it.

## 3. The central result: RTK error is time-correlated, so averaging does not help

Sigma of block-averaged residuals. White noise would fall as 1/√τ; the last
column is what white noise *would* give at τ = 2 s.

| quantity | τ = 0.1 s | τ = 0.5 s | τ = 2 s | white @ 2 s | ratio to white |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw GNSS north | 1.75 mm | 1.25 mm | 0.35 mm | 0.39 mm | **0.9× — white** |
| raw GNSS east | 1.94 mm | 1.31 mm | 1.39 mm | 0.43 mm | **3.2×** |
| raw GNSS altitude | 4.76 mm | 3.65 mm | 3.74 mm | 1.06 mm | **3.5×** |
| raw GNSS velocity N/E/D | 7.9–10.6 mm/s | 2.5–4.7 mm/s | 0.3–0.5 mm/s | 1.8–2.4 mm/s | **0.2× — better than white** |
| raw GNSS yaw | 0.13° | 0.07° | 0.01° | 0.03° | 0.3× |
| EKF local x | 2.07 mm | 1.93 mm | 0.86 mm | 0.46 mm | 1.9× |
| EKF local y | 3.13 mm | 2.98 mm | **3.63 mm** | 0.70 mm | **5.2×** |
| EKF local z | 5.73 mm | 5.50 mm | **6.51 mm** | 1.28 mm | **5.1×** |

Read: **GNSS velocity and yaw average down freely; GNSS east and altitude do
not; the EKF's own y and z do not average down at all** — they are flat or
rising with τ, which is the signature of an error with a correlation time longer
than the window.

This is the quantitative form of the risk P3 already lists: a stable, wrong RTK
solution is not removed by time, and the 125–152 mm power-cycle-cleared vertical
offset is the same phenomenon at a much longer correlation time. **A sigma
measured over a 5 s stop is high-frequency jitter and is not the error that
reaches a marking point.**

Caveat: the τ = 2 s column uses a 3-block minimum on mean-removed windows, which
biases it **low**. Treat these as floors on the correlated component.

### Repeatability at a revisited location

The only external reference these logs contain (per CLAUDE.md: raw-vs-fused
agreement proves nothing; revisiting a physical point does). Stationary windows
clustered within 0.30 m:

| cluster | windows | horizontal spread | vertical range |
|---|---|---|---|
| N+1.23 E−0.00 | 4 (`log_87/89/98/103`) | ≤ 34.8 mm | **42.1 mm** |
| N−0.01 E+6.30 | 2 (`log_87/89`) | ≤ 13.7 mm | 2.6 mm |

Horizontal spread is an **upper bound** — it mixes RTK repeatability with the
rover not parking in exactly the same spot. Vertical spread is the clean signal:
**42 mm at one point across four runs, against a 4.8 mm within-stop jitter — 9×.**

Only post-power-cycle runs form clusters, so this dataset cannot independently
re-derive the `log_79`/`log_82` vertical offset by clustering; the audit's
per-pile method already did. It is however visible in the raw window means —
`log_79` (−222 / −150 mm) and `log_82` (−144 mm) sit far below every
post-power-cycle window (−29 … +55 mm), which is a third independent method
reaching the same conclusion.

| GNSS `heading_accuracy` | 0.2° | 0.128° | 1.6× conservative |
| EKF `eph` | 13.0 mm | ~3.8 mm | 3.4× |
| EKF `epv` | 14.2 mm | 5.7 mm | 2.5× |
| EKF `evh` | 38.9 mm/s | 6.4 mm/s | 6.1× |
| EKF `heading_var` → sigma | 0.5° | 0.0095° | 53× |

Two things follow. The receiver's **position** self-assessment is conservative
against jitter (and, as §3 shows, still optimistic against correlated error — it
reported `fix_type 6` / `h_acc 15 mm` throughout the 150 mm vertical anomaly).
Its **velocity** self-assessment is not usable: 0.4 mm/s is 20× below measured.
PX4 applies `max(reported, EKF2_GPS_V_NOISE)`, so **`EKF2_GPS_V_NOISE = 0.30`
is what is actually protecting the velocity fusion, and it should not be
lowered toward the reported value.**

This closes one Task 2.3 acceptance line ("no repeated GNSS-yaw rejection") for
this dataset, and it closes P3 item 6 for the *rejection* question specifically:

gate has **no practical detection authority** on this vehicle as configured. A
stable, confidently-wrong GNSS solution — exactly the `log_82` vertical failure
mode — arrives with a near-zero innovation and is fused at 100 %. `EKF2_GPS_CHECK
= 831` additionally disables the vertical-drift and horizontal-speed-offset
checks, and `estimator_gps_status` accordingly reports 0/1390 failing samples.
**Nothing in the current configuration would have flagged that fault.**

## 6. New finding: the EKF keeps moving for ~1.7 s after the drivetrain stops

Measured from the untrimmed stop onset, referenced to the last 1.0 s of the same
stop, 36 stops:

| source | displacement at stop onset | peak excursion | time to stay within 10 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| **raw GNSS** | p50 5.7 mm, p95 36.1 mm, max 158.8 mm | p50 9.2 mm | **p50 0.09 s**, max 3.23 s |
| **EKF** | p50 15.9 mm, p95 111.4 mm, max 140.7 mm | p50 30.2 mm | **p50 1.72 s**, max 3.16 s (2 never settled) |

The raw receiver is effectively already settled when the motors go neutral
(p50 0.09 s). The EKF is not: it reports a further **16 mm (p50) to 111 mm
(p95)** of motion and takes **1.72 s (p50)** to come within 10 mm of its own
final answer. The settle-sweep in §2 confirms this independently.

This generalises the note in CLAUDE.md that the EKF along-track estimate runs
"transiently optimistic by ~25 mm and self-corrects over ~1.5 s". Across 36
stops it is p50 16 mm / p95 111 mm over p50 1.72 s.

**Consequence for the marking gate, which is a controller fact, not an estimator
one:** `arrival_settle_sec` is **0.30 s** and `marking_hold_sec` is 3.0 s, while
the terminal zero latch is one-shot. The 30 mm verdict is therefore taken while
the EKF is, at p50, still 16 mm from its own settled position — a bias of the
same order as the 30 mm tolerance being judged. This is consistent with the
2026-09-03 finding that RPP's verdict disagreed with physical truth on 6 of 43
points (3 false accepts, 3 false rejects). **Not changed here** — it belongs to
the stop-accuracy work, and it is recorded so 2.2 does not try to tune it away.

## 7. What this says for Task 2.2 — recommendations only, nothing applied

1. **`EKF2_GPS_P_NOISE = 0.01` is not below measured receiver jitter** (2.6 mm
   horizontal), so it does not violate the P0 rule on the narrow reading. It
   *is* far below the correlated error that actually reaches a point (§3: east
   and altitude 3–3.5× above white at τ = 2 s; 42 mm vertical repeatability at
   one location; 125–152 mm across a power cycle). Whether to raise it is a real
   decision, not an obvious one — raising it slows GNSS authority and buys
   little against a *stable* bias, which is the failure mode actually observed.
   **Recommend deciding this alongside `EKF2_GPS_P_GATE`, not on the sigma alone.**
2. **`EKF2_GPS_V_NOISE = 0.30` should not be lowered.** Measured GNSS velocity
   error is 8–11 mm/s while the receiver reports 0.4 mm/s; `EKF2_GPS_V_NOISE`
   is the only thing standing between the filter and a 20× over-trusted
   velocity observation.
3. **Tightening any gate is pointless as a detector.** Worst observed test ratio
   is 0.143. Gates would have to tighten ~7× before they rejected anything at
   all, and the fault worth catching produces a *near-zero* innovation. Detection
   needs an external reference (revisited points, a second height source, or
   receiver-native ambiguity/base state — P3 item 7), not a gate change.
4. **`EKF2_REQ_GPS_H = 1.0 s`** (firmware default 10 s) is untouched by this
   measurement — it governs acceptance at startup, and no run here started
   degraded. Leave it on the P3 list.
5. **Do not tune from the τ = 0.1 s column.** It is jitter. Anyone reaching for a
   noise parameter should read §3 first.

## 8. Limitations

- 50.8 s of qualifying stationary data in 16 windows; 3 of 8 runs contributed
  ≤ 1 window and `log_77` none. Thin, and mission stops are near the method's
  resolution limit because the marking hold is only 3.0 s.
- Single site (Madhavaram), single afternoon, one base/correction stream. Nothing
  here characterises the receiver across sky conditions or baselines.
- **A proper receiver noise characterisation wants a long static log** — the
  rover parked, armed, RTK FIXED, logging at full rate for 20–30 min. That would
  give the correlation time directly instead of the τ ≤ 2 s floor obtained here,
  and it needs no bench and no mission. **Recommend capturing one** the next time
  the rover is powered, before Task 2.2 is closed.
- Sep-04 estimator internals are absent entirely (no usable ulog). If Sep-04
  behaviour matters, the logging profile needs checking at the rover.
